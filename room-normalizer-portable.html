Room Normalizer · PDP Component Exploration
Concept Index
10 prebuilt rate concepts + 4 composition-based builds exploring organizational and interaction patterns for hotel room and rate presentation in a B2B travel booking flow.

Problem statement

The current PDP displays a long list of room rates with inconsistent naming and overlapping rate plans. Users struggle with duplicate room types listed under different labels, making comparison difficult and reducing confidence in choices.

Solution goal

Implement a Room Normalizer block that organizes messy inventory into intuitive room types, exposes 5–10 bookable rates per group, improves clarity and decision speed, while maintaining direct reservability for every option.

Splits the booking decision into two steps: pick the room, then pick the rate. Room is the primary selector; rates are secondary.

What this is

Per-room table with aligned, sortable columns. Users can sort by any attribute to find what matters to them.

Why it works

Best-in-class for like-for-like comparison. Aligned columns expose subtle differences (e.g. one rate is non-refundable but $30 cheaper) that pill-based rows hide.

Best use case

B2B travel agents and corporate bookers who already know which attributes matter and want to compare them quickly.

Tradeoffs / risks

Feels dense and spreadsheet-like. Mobile-hostile. Less warm than card-based concepts. Long rate-plan names break column alignment.

What this is

Each room type is a soft outer card. Inside, each rate is a smaller bordered tile with attributes and Reserve.

Friendlier feel than a table. Each rate reads as a discrete "offer" rather than a row in a list, reducing spreadsheet fatigue.

What this is

For each room type, one "Recommended" rate renders as a hero block with full detail and a prominent Reserve. Remaining rates collapse behind "+N more."

Why it works

Optimizes for the common path: most users will accept the recommended option. Reduces choice paralysis without removing the long tail.

Best use case

Default booking flows, repeat agents, and policy-driven environments where "the right answer" usually exists.

Tradeoffs / risks

Trust depends entirely on recommendation logic being good. Bad recommendations erode confidence in normalization itself. Hidden rates feel arbitrary without explanation.

Top 2–3 rates shown initially per room group. "Show N more" reveals the rest inline without navigating away.

What this is

Each group leads with a confidence banner showing match count, match quality level, and a "How we matched" panel link.

Why it works

Directly addresses the trust problem in normalization. Users know when to trust the grouping vs. when to scrutinize more carefully.
