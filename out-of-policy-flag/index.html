Out of policy flag — component concepts

The filled flag icon that appears next to price on out-of-policy hotel cards is currently a simple hover tooltip. This explores two interaction directions for the same trigger — a lightweight tooltip and a heavier structured popover — each in small, medium, and large variants. Built as one generic, reusable "Out of policy" component driven by data (line of business + violation reason), not hardcoded to hotel — proven out on both the Hotel and Air SRP card patterns below.

Design decisions (resolved from the working assumptions in this exploration)

Two directions isolate interaction weight as the variable, not content — both tooltip and popover dialog open on hover, click, or tap (the standard interaction for anchored disclosure), differing only in weight: tooltip has no close button and relies on idle-dismiss, popover has an explicit close button plus idle-dismiss as a safety net. A third direction (modal) was explored and then dropped from scope · small/medium/large change only the flag icon's size (16/18/20px) — panel width, padding, and copy (font_300: 14px, title bold, text regular) stay constant across sizes, and the hotel/flight card examples default to the small size · content is data-driven by line of business: format (a flowing sentence vs. a numbered list, 1-2 items) varies, but the title is standardized to "Out of Policy" everywhere — a deliberate call, since real Air and Rail screenshots showed two different live conventions ("Policy violations:" and no title at all) that this unifies — demonstrated live above on both a data preview and a real Air SRP card, Car shown as an explicitly flagged placeholder pending real copy · every hover/focus-revealed panel is closed with blur() on every dismiss path, since :focus-visible alone can keep it visually open after the JS state changes. Full write-up: design-decisions.md.
