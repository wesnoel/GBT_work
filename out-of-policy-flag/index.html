Out of policy flag — component concepts

The filled flag icon that appears next to price on out-of-policy hotel cards is currently a simple hover tooltip. This explores three interaction directions for the same trigger — a lightweight tooltip, a heavier structured popover, and a full modal takeover — each in small, medium, and large variants. Built as one generic, reusable "Out of policy" component driven by data (line of business + violation reason), not hardcoded to hotel.

Design decisions (resolved from the working assumptions in this exploration)

Three directions isolate interaction weight as the variable, not content: tooltip and popover dialog both open on hover, click, or tap (the standard interaction for anchored disclosure going forward), differing in weight — tooltip has no close button and relies on idle-dismiss, popover has an explicit close button plus idle-dismiss as a safety net; modal is click/tap only, explicit dismiss only, no idle timer, since a full takeover should never appear from a stray hover · small/medium/large change only the flag icon's size (16/18/20px) — panel width, padding, and copy (font_300: 14px, title bold, text regular) stay constant across sizes, and the hotel card examples default to the small size · content is data-driven by line of business: format (a flowing sentence vs. a numbered list, 1-2 items) varies, but the title is standardized to "Out of Policy" everywhere — a deliberate call, since real Air and Rail screenshots showed two different live conventions ("Policy violations:" and no title at all) that this unifies — demonstrated live above, Car shown as an explicitly flagged placeholder pending real copy · every hover/focus-revealed panel is closed with blur() on every dismiss path, since :focus-visible alone can keep it visually open after the JS state changes. Full write-up: design-decisions.md.
