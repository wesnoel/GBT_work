Context Keeper is a lightweight personal workspace that holds your place for you when you switch between tasks — and quietly builds a record of what you've accomplished along the way. Here's exactly how it works.
The idea in one line
Every task gets a "context card." Capture where things stand before you switch away — and pick back up in seconds instead of piecing it together from memory, Slack, and old notes.
Using the tool — step by step
1
Start a card in seconds
Click + New card from the home view. It only asks for two things: a title and a quick note about what's going on. That's deliberate — if capturing context takes more than a few seconds, it stops happening.

NoteStar rating renders on desktop, working through partial-star rounding…
2
Capture where things stand
Open any card to fill in the details as you go — not all at once. Update "Where things stand" before you step away, jot down open questions, drop in links (a Slack thread, a Jira ticket, a Figma file), and leave yourself a one-line next step for when you come back.
💡 Think of it as a note to your future self, not documentation for anyone else. It only has to make sense to you.
3
Switch away, and come back without losing your place
Every card carries a status, so the home view always shows exactly where things stand across everything you're juggling.
In Progress Waiting Done
Get pulled into a Slack question or a meeting? No problem — when you come back, open the card and your next step is already sitting there waiting for you.
💡 You can also drag cards to reorder them — put whatever you're actually focused on at the top, regardless of when it was last updated.
4
Mark it done — and tag a win if it's worth remembering
Move a card to Done and you'll get a quick, optional prompt: tag this as a win? Add a short note if something's worth remembering — praise from a teammate, a tricky bug you caught, a fast turnaround.
★ Shipped the fix same day — Priya said it looked great in review.
💡 This step is optional and takes ten seconds. Skip it freely — nothing breaks if you don't tag every card.
5
Pull up your Accomplishment Log before a review
Every card you've marked done lives in one place, with your tagged wins highlighted. By the time a self-review conversation comes around, you're not reconstructing your quarter from memory — you're reading it back.
Quick answers
Do I need to fill in every field on a card?
No. Title and a quick note are all it takes to start. Open questions, links, and next step are there when they're useful — never required.
Does this connect to Slack or Jira automatically?
Not yet. Right now you paste in links by hand. That's intentional for this prototype — automatic syncing is a natural next step once the core habit proves out.
What happens to my data?
Everything stays in your own browser for this prototype — nothing is sent anywhere. It's built to feel private, the same way you'd treat a personal notebook.
What if I don't get around to updating a card right away?
Nothing breaks — a card just sits at whatever status you last left it. The tool is meant to lower effort, not add a new obligation to track.
Where this stands today

This is a hackathon prototype, built for one person's workflow — not a finished product. Entry is manual by design, there's no team view, and nothing syncs live with Slack or Jira yet.

If the core idea proves out — that capturing context in seconds actually sticks as a habit — the natural next steps are lightweight integrations and, eventually, opening it up beyond a single person.
